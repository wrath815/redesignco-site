## Core idea

United Carriers does not present logistics as a list of services. It turns the shipment itself into the narrative. The homepage carries one idea from loading to road transport, ocean freight, and air freight, so the business story and the interaction story are the same thing.

The agency describes the experience as a continuous sequence rather than a stack of disconnected sections. That is the primary design decision, and the Three.js scenes exist to support it rather than to act as decoration.

## Visual language

The visual system is industrial and high-contrast: deep blue, white space, freight machinery, containers, vehicles, cranes, and a large typographic voice. The site uses real operational objects as the visual identity instead of generic stock photography. Product-like isolated renders and wide cinematic scenes let the visitor understand the physical system at a glance.

The live page balances spectacle with restrained information sections. The hero and journey sequence earn attention; the services, reliability, FAQ, and quote path make the experience useful for an actual logistics buyer.

## Interaction system

Awwwards identifies the project with 3D, Three.js, GSAP, Webflow, microinteractions, responsive behavior, transitions, and storytelling. The featured elements include a preloader, an interactive 3D hero, a storytelling sequence, a scroll-to-explore industries section, page transitions, a services page, and a custom 404 page.

The important lesson is not “add Three.js.” It is “give motion a job.” The animation makes the freight journey legible, establishes pacing, and keeps the many service areas inside one mental model.

## Content and conversion

The first message is unusually clear: “One operator / Every leg of the journey.” The supporting copy immediately explains the commercial promise: freight forwarding, land transport, and customs brokerage under one accountable team.

The page then proves the promise with concrete numbers: 2,500+ shipments per month, a 98.2% on-time delivery rate, and 8+ years in operation. Services are grouped under one operator, reliability is framed around real-time tracking, network coverage, and 24/7 support, and the page ends with FAQ and a direct “work with us” path.

This is a strong sequence for a B2B business: emotional explanation first, operational proof second, service detail third, friction removal fourth, contact action last.

## What RedesignCo should take

RedesignCo should borrow the structural principle, not the freight aesthetic. A redesign concept should have one dominant metaphor that carries the visitor through the page. For example, “from overlooked to chosen” could move a business from first impression to trust, proof, and enquiry. Each interaction should advance that transformation.

The current RedesignCo page has atmosphere and motion, but its sections can still feel like separate demonstrations. The United Carriers lesson is to make the sections feel causally connected: the next visual state should be the consequence of the previous one.

Use real business proof earlier. The site’s metrics work because they appear after the story has created interest. RedesignCo can use a compact proof moment after the hero: measurable project outcomes where available, otherwise explicitly labelled process guarantees rather than invented claims.

Use imagery as a system. United Carriers does not use one hero image and then abandon the visual idea; vehicles, containers, routes, and machinery recur throughout the experience. RedesignCo should similarly choose a small family of visual motifs for each concept and carry them through the page.

Keep the service area calmer than the hero. The most spectacular part should attract attention, while the services and contact path should become easier to scan. More spectacle is not automatically more premium.

## Do not copy

Do not copy the freight journey, specific scenes, blue industrial palette, wording, 3D models, or transition choreography. Do not add WebGL merely to imitate an award-winning implementation. The transferable asset is the narrative architecture: one metaphor, one continuous thread, and motion that clarifies the business.

## Verdict

United Carriers is valuable to RedesignCo mainly as a lesson in narrative cohesion. Its best move is not the 3D rendering. Its best move is making a complex B2B service feel like one understandable journey, then backing that journey with proof and a practical conversion path.
